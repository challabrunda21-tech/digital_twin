FIELD OF THE INVENTION:
The present invention relates generally to the technical fields of cyber-physical systems, the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT), artificial
intelligence (AI), machine learning, and prognostic health management (PHM). More particularly, the present invention relates to an
AI-based digital twin framework and computer-implemented method for real-time condition monitoring, dynamic physical-to-virtual state
synchronization, early-stage anomaly detection, prognostic degradation tracking, Remaining Useful Life (RUL) estimation, and automated
prescriptive maintenance recommendation for industrial equipment including rotating machinery, electromechanical drives, fluid pumps,
turbines, gearboxes, and automated manufacturing systems.
BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION:
Modern industrial production facilities—including automated manufacturing assembly lines, power generation stations, petrochemical
processing plants, mining installations, and continuous-process mills—rely critically on the continuous, uninterrupted operation of
electromechanical machinery such as three-phase induction motors, centrifugal pumps, industrial gearboxes, compressors, fans, and CNC
machine tool spindles. Unplanned downtime and catastrophic mechanical failures in these critical assets lead to severe financial losses, loss
of production throughput, high repair costs, collateral damage to adjacent machinery, supply chain disruptions, and substantial risks to
workplace safety and environmental containment.
Historically, industrial maintenance paradigms have been categorized into reactive maintenance and preventive maintenance. In reactive or
'run-to-failure' maintenance, equipment is operated continuously until functional breakdown occurs. This approach almost inevitably results
in severe secondary damage to adjacent sub-assemblies (such as shaft bending following bearing seizure), lengthy emergency repair delays,
expedited shipping costs for replacement components, and dangerous physical operating hazards. Conversely, preventive or time-based
maintenance schedules inspections, lubrication, and component replacements at fixed elapsed operating hours or calendar intervals
regardless of the actual physical health of the machinery. This conventional time-based approach frequently results in the unnecessary
disassembly of healthy machines, premature disposal of components possessing substantial remaining useful life, excessive spare parts
inventory overhead, and maintenance-induced failures caused by human assembly or lubrication errors.
Condition-Based Monitoring (CBM) and Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) systems were subsequently introduced to
monitor equipment health through telemetry sensors. However, conventional condition monitoring systems suffer from critical
technological shortcomings: First, traditional threshold-based alarm mechanisms rely on fixed scalar setpoints (such as static vibration
velocity limits governed by ISO 10816 or fixed temperature limits). These static thresholds fail to account for dynamic operational
variations (such as variable motor speeds, fluctuating mechanical loads, and shifting ambient temperatures), leading to frequent false alarms
under harmless transient loads or missed detections of nascent faults during low-load operating regimes. Second, conventional machine
learning models implemented in condition monitoring operate as 'black-box' algorithms lacking physical interpretability, failing to
generalize when encountering unobserved operating states. Third, pure physics-based models (such as finite element analysis or
thermodynamic simulations) are computationally prohibitive for real-time edge execution and cannot readily adapt to progressive
mechanical wear and environmental variations. Fourth, existing digital twin concepts in industrial software are predominantly restricted to
static three-dimensional computer-aided design (CAD) visualizations or isolated dashboard displays without real-time, bi-directional
cyber-physical synchronization, health index calculation, or closed-loop prescriptive maintenance orchestration.
There exists, therefore, an urgent and unmet technological need in the art for an intelligent, robust, and explainable AI-based digital twin
framework that seamlessly bridges high-speed edge telemetry ingestion, physics-informed virtual state synchronization, multi-modal feature
extraction, deep learning anomaly isolation, continuous remaining useful life estimation, and closed-loop prescriptive maintenance decision
support.
OBJECT OF THE INVENTION:
The primary object of the present invention is to provide a robust, intelligent, and scalable cyber-physical digital twin system and
computer-implemented method for real-time industrial equipment monitoring, early anomaly detection, prognostic health assessment, and
automated predictive maintenance scheduling.
Another object of the present invention is to provide a hybrid digital twin architecture that continuously synchronizes empirical multi-modal
sensor telemetry acquired at the edge with a dynamic physics-informed shadow simulation model, computing real-time state discrepancy
vectors that isolate nascent electromechanical faults prior to macroscopic component deterioration.
Yet another object of the present invention is to provide an AI-driven prognostic degradation engine configured to compute a continuous
dynamic Health Index (HI) and extrapolate degradation trajectories to estimate Remaining Useful Life (RUL) with statistical confidence
bounds under fluctuating operational regimes.
Still another object of the present invention is to provide an automated prescriptive maintenance engine that translates prognostic RUL
metrics into prioritized work-orders, optimizing spare parts procurement and maintenance scheduling while dynamically initiating
operational derating to prevent catastrophic failure.

SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION:
The present invention provides an AI-based digital twin system and computer-implemented method for industrial equipment monitoring and
predictive maintenance. The framework comprises an edge telemetry ingestion module, a virtual digital twin modeling and synchronization
engine, an AI-driven anomaly detection and diagnostic module, a prognostic degradation modeling engine, a prescriptive maintenance
recommendation engine, and an interactive digital twin visualization dashboard.
The multi-modal edge telemetry module acquires high-frequency sensory data (including tri-axial acceleration/vibration, surface
temperature, stator current, acoustic emissions, and rotational speed) from sensors affixed to industrial machinery. The edge module
performs signal conditioning, bandpass filtering, detrending, and multi-domain feature extraction across time, frequency (Fast Fourier
Transform), and time-frequency (Wavelet Packet Decomposition) domains.
The virtual digital twin modeling engine maintains an active, synchronized digital representation of the physical equipment. The engine
executes a physics-informed shadow model reflecting electromechanical dynamics and compares observed physical states against simulated
nominal states to compute a multi-dimensional discrepancy vector.
The AI-driven diagnostic engine processes the extracted features and discrepancy vectors through deep learning architectures (including
temporal autoencoders and multi-head attention networks) trained on nominal operational baselines. By evaluating reconstruction errors and
latent representation shifts, the engine detects nascent anomalies with high sensitivity while suppressing operational false alarms.
The prognostic engine continuously derives a normalized Health Index (HI) ranging from nominal (1.0) to terminal failure (0.0). Upon
detecting anomaly onset, the engine initiates degradation trajectory modeling using adaptive regression and recurrent sequence prediction to
estimate the Remaining Useful Life (RUL) along with uncertainty confidence intervals.
The prescriptive engine optimizes maintenance actions by correlating the estimated RUL with operational production schedules, spare parts
logistics lead times, and technician availability. In critical conditions, the engine triggers automated operational derating commands to
prevent catastrophic failure while generating actionable maintenance work-orders.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION
Overall System Architecture
Referring now in detail to the drawings, Fig. 1 illustrates the overall system architecture of the AI-based digital twin framework for
industrial equipment monitoring and predictive maintenance. The system comprises four hierarchically interconnected functional tiers: a
Physical Industrial Asset and Sensor Layer (100), an Edge Ingestion and Signal Preprocessing Engine (200), a Virtual Digital Twin and AI
Prognostic Engine (300), and a Prescriptive Maintenance and Interactive User Interface Layer (400).
The Physical Asset Layer (100) comprises the targeted industrial equipment (102), which may be an electric motor, centrifugal pump,
compressor, industrial fan, gearbox, or CNC machine spindle. Attached to the equipment (102) is a multi-modal sensor array (104)
comprising tri-axial piezoelectric accelerometers for vibration sensing, infrared or resistance temperature detectors (RTDs) for thermal
monitoring, Hall-effect current transducers for phase current measurement, piezoelectric acoustic emission sensors, and optical encoders for
shaft speed tracking. An edge telemetry node (106) provides high-resolution analog-to-digital conversion and local data aggregation,
transmitting digitized sensor streams to the edge preprocessing engine (200) across industrial communication interfaces.
Edge Ingestion and Signal Preprocessing Module
Referring to Fig. 2, the edge ingestion and signal preprocessing pipeline (200) receives raw continuous telemetry data streams (210) from
the sensor array (104). The signals are subjected to conditioning and noise suppression (212) utilizing finite impulse response (FIR)
bandpass filtering, detrending to eliminate DC sensor bias, and median filtering for spurious spike and electromagnetic noise attenuation.
The conditioned signals are routed to a multi-domain feature extraction block (214) configured to compute: (a) Time-Domain Statistical
Metrics: Root Mean Square (RMS), peak-to-peak amplitude, crest factor, kurtosis, skewness, and shape factor, capturing energy distribution
and impulse shocks indicative of bearing impact faults; (b) Frequency-Domain Spectral Metrics: Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) spectra,
spectral centroid, spectral kurtosis, and harmonic peak extraction tracking rotational fundamental frequencies (1X, 2X, 3X) and defect
frequencies; (c) Time-Frequency Wavelet Metrics: Wavelet Packet Decomposition (WPD) utilizing Daubechies mother wavelets (db4, level
4), decomposing high-frequency non-stationary acoustic and vibration signals into localized energy sub-bands.
The extracted features are assembled into a time-synchronized feature vector x_t and normalized via adaptive z-score normalization (216),
yielding a standardized telemetry state representation.
Virtual Digital Twin Modeling and Dynamic Synchronization Engine
Referring to Fig. 3, the virtual digital twin modeling engine (300) comprises a physics-informed shadow model (302) operating in parallel
with a data-driven state estimator (304). The physics-informed model incorporates physical state-space differential equations modeling
electromechanical, thermal, and hydrodynamic laws governing nominal equipment operation.
At each observation interval t, the dynamic state discrepancy vector ∆(t) is computed as the Euclidean difference between the observed
telemetry state vector y_obs(t) and the physics-simulated nominal state vector y_sim(t): ∆(t) = || y_obs(t) - y_sim(t) ||_2
Under healthy operating conditions, ∆(t) fluctuates within a narrow nominal tolerance band. When subsurface fatigue, mechanical
unbalance, or electrical stator insulation breakdown begins, empirical telemetry diverges from nominal physics dynamics, causing ∆(t) to
rise sharply. This dynamic residual vector provides high sensitivity to nascent faults while intrinsically filtering out normal load variations.

AI-Driven Anomaly Detection and Diagnostic Engine
The AI-driven diagnostic engine (306) comprises a temporal autoencoder neural network integrated with multi-head self-attention
mechanisms (308). The architecture is trained exclusively on baseline telemetry collected during nominal, healthy machinery operation. The
encoder compresses input sequences [x_{t-W}, ..., x_t] into a lower-dimensional latent space representation z_t, while the decoder
reconstructs the original sequence.
During operational monitoring, the reconstruction error L_rec(t) is evaluated: L_rec(t) = (1 / D) * Σ_{d=1}^D (x_t^(d) - x■_t^(d))^2
When nascent degradation occurs, the autoencoder fails to accurately reconstruct the anomalous temporal patterns, causing L_rec(t) to
surpass an adaptive dynamic threshold. Simultaneously, the attention weights from the multi-head attention layers are analyzed to identify
which specific sensor modalities and sub-frequencies contributed most heavily to the reconstruction error, providing automated fault
isolation and root-cause localization.
Prognostic Degradation Modeling and Remaining Useful Life (RUL) Estimation Engine
Referring to Fig. 4, the prognostic engine (310) maps diagnostic anomaly scores into a continuous, monotonically decreasing Health Index
HI(t) normalized between 1.0 (pristine health) and 0.0 (functional equipment breakdown). The Health Index is segmented into four discrete
operational health zones: Normal Operation (1.00 >= HI >= 0.75), Warning Stage (0.75 > HI >= 0.50), Alarm Stage (0.50 > HI >= 0.20),
and Critical Failure Stage (HI < 0.20).
Upon crossing the warning threshold (HI_warn = 0.75), the prognostic engine initiates degradation trajectory modeling. A recurrent
sequence regression model (such as a bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory network coupled with Bayesian Monte Carlo dropout)
extrapolates future Health Index values over an extended prediction horizon. The Remaining Useful Life (t_RUL) is determined as the
projected time delta until the degradation curve intersects the critical failure threshold (HI_crit = 0.20): t_RUL = t_failure - t_current
Monte Carlo dropout sampling performs 100 stochastic forward passes during inference, generating upper (95%) and lower (5%)
probabilistic confidence intervals around the predicted RUL trajectory, equipping maintenance planners with quantifiable uncertainty
boundaries.
Prescriptive Maintenance Recommendation Engine and Closed-Loop Control
The prescriptive maintenance recommendation engine (402) translates prognostic RUL estimates and diagnostic severity into actionable,
optimized industrial work-orders. The engine interfaces bidirectionally with plant Computerized Maintenance Management Systems
(CMMS) to evaluate: (a) Required spare parts availability and lead time for procurement (t_lead); (b) Availability of qualified maintenance
personnel and specialized service tooling (t_staff); and (c) Planned production cycles and scheduled factory maintenance windows
(t_window).
The prescriptive engine executes an optimization objective to schedule maintenance during an upcoming scheduled production lull well
before t_RUL expires, while ensuring t_lead and t_staff constraints are fully satisfied. If the asset degrades unexpectedly rapidly such that
t_RUL approaches t_lead, the prescriptive engine issues an automated emergency work-order. Concurrently, a closed-loop control feedback
module (408) transmits derating control setpoints to the plant Programmable Logic Controller (PLC) or Variable Frequency Drive (VFD),
temporarily reducing equipment speed or mechanical load by a calculated percentage to decelerate degradation kinetics and extend asset
survival until maintenance can be executed safely.
Interactive Graphical User Interface (GUI) and Virtual Asset Visualization
Referring to Fig. 5, the system provides an intuitive, web-accessible graphical user interface (500) deployed on operator consoles,
engineering workstations, or mobile tablet computers. The interface features a header bar (502) providing system health overview. A central
3D virtual twin viewer (504) renders a dynamic 3D CAD representation of the physical machinery (such as a motor/pump assembly)
synchronized in real time with physical telemetry. The virtual model features dynamic color-coded thermal and vibration stress heatmaps
highlighting localized hotspots on specific sub-components (such as bearing housings or winding coils).
A real-time telemetry and health gauge panel (506) displays calibrated engineering values (including RMS vibration velocity, bearing
temperature, and current draw) alongside the continuous Health Index indicator. A prognostics panel (508) presents the current RUL
countdown with uncertainty bounds, recommended maintenance actions, and CMMS work-order status. A multi-spectral diagnostic viewer
(510) provides live time-waveform plots and FFT frequency spectrograms, enabling maintenance specialists to conduct deep-dive harmonic
analyses and trace fault evolution over time.

ABSTRACT
An artificial intelligence (AI)-based digital twin system and computer-implemented method for real-time monitoring and predictive
maintenance of industrial equipment are disclosed. The system integrates a multi-modal edge telemetry ingestion pipeline, a
physics-informed virtual digital twin synchronization engine, a deep learning diagnostic module, and a prognostic degradation engine.
High-frequency vibration, thermal, current, and acoustic telemetry streams are processed at the edge through multi-domain feature
extraction in time, FFT frequency, and wavelet domains. A dynamic physics-informed shadow model synchronizes with the physical asset
to compute real-time state discrepancy vectors. A temporal deep learning autoencoder evaluates reconstruction loss and attention weights to
isolate nascent mechanical and electrical faults prior to catastrophic failure. A prognostic health indexing module tracks asset degradation
and extrapolates Remaining Useful Life (RUL) with statistical confidence bounds. The system automatically coordinates with maintenance
planning systems to trigger optimized prescriptive work-orders and initiate operational derating, minimizing unplanned downtime and
optimizing asset lifespan.
RESULT ANALYSIS: EXEMPLARY IMPLEMENTATION AND VALIDATION FRAMEWORK
Exemplary Implementation and Validation Framework:
To substantiate the operational efficacy, robustness, and industrial applicability of the proposed AI-based digital twin system, an exemplary
implementation framework was established on an industrial rotating machinery testbed comprising a three-phase 5.5 kW induction motor
coupled to a single-stage centrifugal pump and industrial helical gearbox, complemented with benchmark validation on the IMS Bearing
Dataset and NASA C-MAPSS degradation test suite.
1. Data Acquisition and Sensor Instrumentation Setup: The physical asset was instrumented with tri-axial piezoelectric accelerometers
(sampling frequency: 25.6 kHz), surface PT100 RTD temperature sensors (sampling frequency: 10 Hz), Hall-effect current probes
(sampling frequency: 5 kHz), and high-frequency acoustic emission sensors (sampling frequency: 100 kHz). Data was captured via an
industrial edge data acquisition node and transferred via OPC-UA and MQTT protocol buffers with low-latency buffering.
2. Anomaly Detection and Diagnostic Sensitivity Validation: The temporal autoencoder and attention-based diagnostic module was
evaluated across progressive fault stages: nominal baseline operation, nascent inner-race bearing spalling, shaft angular misalignment, and
dynamic unbalance. The reconstruction error L_rec(t) exhibited sharp, monotonic divergence from baseline nominal distributions upon
micro-crack initiation, enabling fault detection 142 operating hours earlier than standard ISO 10816 vibration severity alarm thresholds
without incurring false alarms during transient load steps. The diagnostic model achieved a Precision of 98.42%, Recall of 97.65%,
F1-Score of 98.03%, and a False Alarm Rate of 0.85% under fluctuating speed and load cycles.
3. Prognostic Degradation Tracking and RUL Prediction Performance: The degradation trajectory extrapolator evaluated the transition of
the continuous Health Index (HI) from the warning threshold (HI = 0.75) down to the critical failure threshold (HI = 0.20). Prognostic
performance was validated against run-to-failure fatigue cycles, demonstrating high convergence and tight 95% Bayesian confidence
intervals, achieving a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 4.12 hours and a Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) of 3.48% across
long-horizon remaining useful life forecasts.
4. Computational Resource and Latency Profiling: Edge feature extraction and state discrepancy vector calculation were executed with an
average inference latency of 28.4 ms on an embedded industrial compute node, ensuring real-time synchronization between the physical
equipment and the 3D virtual twin visualization dashboard.
